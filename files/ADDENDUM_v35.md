# LUMON_TERMINAL — v3.5 addendum

Adds the 10° display rake and the internal-PSU power bay. Generated by
`lumon_v35.py`. Envelope unchanged: **359 × 153 × 144 mm**.

---

## 1. Where the aesthetic details land

The split is not "nice things last", it is whether the detail moves a
mounting face.

**Form-defining — done now, in v3.5.** Rake, and by extension the front
plane's position at every height. Everything that mounts to that plane —
window lip, locating ribs, retainer bosses, retainer frame — is generated in
a local upright frame and then raked as a unit, so they stay coplanar by
construction rather than by hand-fitting.

**Surface — M4.** Fillets and chamfers on the top and front edges, shadow gap
at the seam, bezel chamfer, vent slot styling, ID plate, status LED, feet.
These only cut material from surfaces that are already fixed.

Corner radii sit awkwardly across the two: the external radius is currently
6 mm and is a surface detail, but going much above ~8 mm starts eating the
space the corner bosses occupy. Treat 8 mm as the ceiling unless the bosses
move first.

## 2. What the rake cost

- Front face leans back **25 mm** over the 144 mm height.
- The display's vertical extent shrinks to 110.76 × cos 10° = **109.1 mm**.
  That is inherent to tilting, not a modelling artefact.
- The display's rear face sweeps backwards as it rises: **y ≈ 29 mm at
  z = 28**, **y ≈ 49 mm at the top**. Three things had to move out of that
  swept volume: the mains barrier (now starts at y = 40), the PSU's front
  corner blocks (shortened to 6 mm), and the front pair of top-plate bosses
  (now at y = 58, behind the display's top rear edge). The top plate's front
  edge is located by the rebate ledge instead of by screws.
- Side stiffening flanges are clipped to start behind the display assembly.

### The seam step

The printer module cannot be raked. Its lid opens straight up and its front
sits 3 mm behind the front face, so raking that module would drive the wall
into the printer. The result is a **25 mm step at the top of the seam**: the
display console leans back, the printer block stands vertical.

This is defensible — it reads as a raked console beside a forward equipment
block, which is what the render implies anyway. But it is a real decision, not
a side effect. At M4 it gets resolved deliberately: either a chamfer running
the step into the seam, or a shadow gap that declares it. If you would rather
they align, the alternative is moving the printer back ~25 mm, which pushes
the depth to about 178 mm.

## 3. Power architecture

One 24 V open-frame supply, one mains entry, one buck for the Pi.

```
 IEC C14 (fused + switched) ─ fixed rear wall
        │
        └─ 24 V PSU ─┬─ 24 V ──► printer  (through the spine pass-through)
                     └─ buck ── 5 V ─────► Pi ──► display (GPIO / USB)
```

- **PSU pocket 129 × 97 × 30** at x = 66, y = 40. Sized for Mean Well
  LRS-100-24 (4.5 A, 108 W); the LRS-75-24 (99 × 97 × 30, 3.2 A) drops into
  the same pocket with longer corner blocks. Captured by printed corner
  blocks, not screwed — its own M4 holes stay unused and unmeasured.
- Pulled 13 mm forward of the rear wall so the rear-panel insert bosses clear
  it. They collided in the first pass.
- **Mains inlet in the fixed strip of the rear wall**, not in the removable
  service panel, so mains wiring never crosses a joint you open for service.
  Cutout 47.2 × 28.3 for a fused + switched snap-in C14; the outer face is
  thinned locally to 1.5 mm, because snap-in clips will not grip 3 mm.
- **Mains barrier** at x = 59, 46 mm tall, separating the PSU zone from the
  Pi and buck.
- **Pi mounted vertically** on the left wall on four 58 × 49 standoffs, ports
  facing rear. Flat on the floor it would not fit beside a 129 mm PSU.
- **Ventilation:** intake slots in the floor under the PSU, exhaust high in
  the rear wall. Nothing on any visible surface.

### Sizing caveat

108 W assumes a 24 V printer. **Read the voltage and current off the label of
the printer's own brick at M0** — that is the sizing input and it is currently
a guess. If the printer turns out to be 12 V, the whole rail changes.

Mains inside a printed enclosure: PETG or ASA, not PLA. Terminal block
covered, inlet earthed to the PSU chassis terminal, mains conductors strain
relieved and kept on their own side of the barrier.

## 4. Assembly order change

The spine screw at (y 140, z 45) sits above the PSU and must be driven
**before** the PSU goes in. Splitting the modules later means removing the PSU
first.
